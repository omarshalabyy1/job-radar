# The ultimate guide to Grok Bot for creators

Source: pasted article by @Ethan_Ng_13. The author says "we" use Grok Bot internally for ads, so treat the results (10 videos edited in about 2 hours, three fully AI-produced videos) as the author's own claims, not independently verified.

## Core idea
Bot handles the busywork (research, scripting help, editing, posting, analytics). You keep the creative work: ideas and stories.

## 1. Build a Bot that knows you
- Brain-dump how you make content (voice dictation is fastest). Expect a long session, not five minutes.
- Share past videos (editing style), content you love (why it works), and analytics (so it improves scripts and strategy).
- Also share scripts, writing, stories, lessons and feedback.

## 2. Inspiration into ideas
Bot watches videos you save, files them in Notion, Google Docs or wherever you plan, and breaks each down:
- Why it worked
- The hook (first few seconds)
- Pacing
- Sound (song used)
- How to make it yours

## 3. Script coach (not ghostwriter)
Feed it videos you find engaging plus your own top performers. Then every script gets scored, with drop-off points flagged and fixes given, e.g. "hook is too slow", "you start teaching too early", "tension drops here", "this doesn't sound like you". A rough draft in gives a stronger hook and tighter pacing out.

## 4. Produce entire videos
Claimed examples: a Minecraft-style explainer and a "what if" natural-disaster video (both from a one-sentence prompt: storyboard, script, narration, original music), plus a talking-head ad (best takes cut, animations, overlays, music).

Tools named: transcription (understand footage), Remotion (build the edit), Cursor (complex motion).

Workflow:
1. Train it on your editing style from finished videos; iterate until it explains your style back.
2. Upload raw footage (even a 10-minute take).
3. Get an editing plan (structure, cuts, B-roll, photos from iCloud or Drive).
4. Approve or give feedback before any editing starts.
5. Kick off the edit: in CapCut, DaVinci Resolve or Premiere Pro, or on the Bot's own cloud computer (recommended).
6. Review, leave notes, final changes.
7. Post.

## 5. Parallel creation
Its own cloud computer lets it edit many videos at once. Claimed test: 10 raw files edited in about 2 hours, versus about 2 hours for one video by hand, after a few rounds of feedback.

## 6. Every video teaches the next
Connect analytics from every platform. Bot tracks retention drops, compares skip and share rates, and forms hypotheses. Example breakdown for a drop at 0:18: started explaining instead of moving the story, shot never changed, open loop already resolved, so lesson is to keep visual and story tension through the explanation. Lessons feed the next script.

## 7. Post once, post everywhere
Give it the finished video, caption and hashtags, connect accounts, and it posts from its cloud computer to X, YouTube, Instagram and TikTok. The article also mentions the Video Kickstart bonus program.

## Flywheel
Learn, find inspiration, generate ideas, script, record, edit, distribute, analyze, repeat.

## Notes for use
- Posting to your accounts is outward-facing. Per the one-person-company guide, keep a manual approval step before anything publishes.
- Connected social and analytics accounts widen what the shared Bot computer can reach. See the access rules in `grok-bot-one-person-company.md`.
