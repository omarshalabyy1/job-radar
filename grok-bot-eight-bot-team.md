# "I replaced my whole team with 8 Grok Bots": the eight bots and prompts

Source: pasted article by a solo founder who also sells a newsletter (The AI Headstart) and community seats, so treat it as promotional. Claims (hours cut from 8 to under 3, 15 to 20x salary, 5 businesses, 0 employees) are the author's own and unverified. The author's progression was OpenClaw, then Hermes, then Grok Bot.

## Core rules
1. **One bot, one narrow job, one schedule.** A bot given two jobs got worse at both. A bot with no role is just a slower general chat app.
2. **Bots draft, you decide.** Build the approval gate into each prompt. The author removed one as an experiment and got a week of output to undo.
3. Add bots in the order the pain demands, over weeks. Start with the most annoying recurring task, not the most impressive one.
4. Don't automate judgment; automate the walking, clicking, scanning and drafting around it. The bottleneck becomes taste: "is this good enough to ship?"

## The eight bots (prompts quoted from the article, lightly condensed)
1. **Content Scout.** Every morning at 7am, scan X and Reddit for the 3 biggest AI waves from the last 24 hours. For each: link, why it's blowing up, one angle to post. One message, then wait for your reply before drafting anything. (The "wait for my reply" line stops bots getting eager. When its X credits ran out it paused, said what it needed, and resumed. Planned upgrade: Gmail, Typefully and Higgsfield access, writing style loaded, finished draft plus visual dropped into Typefully for review.)
2. **Product Explorer.** Watch the inbox for sponsor and collab pitches. When one lands, explore the product fully; if an account is needed, ask you to sign in once on its computer. Build one small real thing, stress test it, then report what it does, what surprised it, what it's good at, and a verdict: worth my audience or skip. "Be brutal. My audience's trust is the budget."
3. **Mobile App QA.** Walk through the app like a brand-new user who reads nothing; note where it gets stuck, confused or errors. Ask before testing anything that costs money. Report as a list, worst problem first. (If it can't find the entry point, neither can a user.)
4. **AI Community.** Writes welcome emails, weekly posts, announcement drafts; drafts only, checks in with "as-is or rewrite?" Full prompt is behind the author's newsletter.
5. **IG Sponsor.** Holds each brand's requirements and preps frames, hooks and captions to the brief before recording.
6. **Invoices.** Keep a ledger of every forwarded deal (brand, amount, terms, due date). When late, draft a short polite follow-up and remind every 3 days until paid. Never send anything itself; escalate tone only when told.
7. **Client Research.** Given a new client's name and industry: top 5 competitors, what users complain about in reviews, what the MVP must include, 3 kickoff-call questions. One one-page report before the call.
8. **Chief of Staff.** The one bot you talk to; routes work to specialists in group chats and chases updates, ending with one question for you. Still being onboarded at time of writing.

## Watch-outs (from the article)
- Expect lots of questions at first; answer properly and they stop repeating them.
- All bots share one cloud computer: any login or file given to one is available to all. Treat it as giving keys to the whole team. Screenshots in chats contain real client and sponsor details.
- Vague jobs burn credits; narrow roles are also cost control.
- Review routines weekly; a bot that drifted on Monday stays drifted.
